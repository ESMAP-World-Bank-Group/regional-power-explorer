Generation & Capacity Mix

Romania's generation mix is among the most diversified in the EU, combining firm low-carbon output (nuclear and large hydro) with a still-significant thermal baseload and a rapidly growing variable renewables fleet. Total installed capacity reached approximately 24 GW in 2024, producing around 60 TWh of electricity.

Market Structure & Regulation

Romania is an EU member since 2007 and operates within the ENTSO-E synchronous European grid. The electricity market is formally liberalised at wholesale level, though household retail tariffs retain partial regulation.

Regulatory & institutional framework

  • ANRE — National Energy Regulatory Authority; sets tariffs, licenses, and market rules.
  • Transelectrica — state-owned TSO; operates the 400 kV and 220 kV transmission network and manages system balancing.
  • OPCOM / BRM — power exchange and day-ahead/intra-day market operator; integrated into EUPHEMIA price coupling.
  • DSOs — Electrica (state-controlled, northern and central Romania), E.ON Energie România (Moldova region), E-Distribuție (formerly Enel, acquired; south and west).
Romania's wholesale electricity market is coupled with the European day-ahead market via EUPHEMIA, enabling cross-border price arbitrage with Hungary, Bulgaria, and Serbia. However, congestion on key cross-border lines frequently limits the benefits of market integration.

Key Generating Assets

Nuclear — Cernavodă NPP

Romania operates two CANDU-6 pressurised heavy-water reactors at Cernavodă, on the Danube-Black Sea Canal in Constanța County. Unit 1 (706 MW) was commissioned in 1996 and Unit 2 (706 MW) in 2007, operated by SNN (Societatea Națională Nuclearelectrica), a state-majority company listed on the Bucharest Stock Exchange. The two units together produce roughly 11 TWh per year — about 18–19% of national electricity.

Units 3 and 4 at Cernavodă (~1 400 MW combined, also CANDU-6 design) have been partially constructed since the 1980s. After decades of stalled negotiations, a new intergovernmental agreement with the United States was signed in 2020, with AtkinsRéalis (formerly SNC-Lavalin) and US nuclear partners engaged under an EPC contract framework signed in 2024. Target commissioning is approximately 2031–2035 at an estimated cost of €7–8 billion. Completion would make Romania the largest nuclear electricity producer in Southeast Europe.

Hydropower — Hidroelectrica

Hidroelectrica operates approximately 6 400 MW of installed hydro capacity across more than 200 hydropower plants on the Olt, Argeș, Lotru, and other river systems, as well as the Iron Gates I and II plants on the Danube (co-owned with Serbia). It is the backbone of Romania's dispatchable renewable generation. In June 2023, Hidroelectrica completed the largest IPO in Romanian history on the Bucharest Stock Exchange (BVB), raising approximately €1.9 billion and creating Romania's most valuable listed company. The state retains a majority stake via Fondul Proprietatea.

Coal — CE Oltenia

CE Oltenia integrates lignite mining in the Oltenia basin (Gorj, Vâlcea counties) with approximately 3 GW of thermal generation at Rovinari, Turceni, and Ișalnița power stations. The company employs roughly 15 000 people directly and anchors the local economy of several counties. Under EU ETS pressure and Romania's Just Transition commitment, CE Oltenia is undergoing a restructuring plan backed by the Just Transition Fund and European Commission state-aid approvals, targeting a significant reduction in coal capacity by 2030–2032 with partial replacement by gas peakers and renewables on existing sites.

Interconnections & Trade

Romania is synchronously interconnected within the ENTSO-E Continental Europe (CE) synchronous area and shares borders — and high-voltage lines — with five countries. It is typically a net electricity exporter, leveraging its low-cost hydro and nuclear baseload.

🇧🇬
Bulgaria Net export
The Romania–Bulgaria corridor via the 400 kV Dobrudja lines is Romania's most important export route. Surplus hydro and nuclear output flows south, particularly in wet years. The corridor also links to the Southeast European electricity market and Greece.
🇲🇩
Moldova Strategic
Romania supplies emergency and commercial electricity to Moldova, which disconnected from the Russian/IPS grid in 2022. The B2B converter at Vulcănești is a strategic asset; a new direct 400 kV HVAC line between the two countries is in planning to enable full synchronous integration and support Moldova's EU accession energy reforms.
🇺🇦
Ukraine Emergency sync
Since March 2022, Ukraine and Moldova operate in emergency synchronisation with the ENTSO-E CE area (initially via Romania and Slovakia). Romania's Isaccea B2B station and transmission infrastructure play a key role in supporting Ukraine's electricity system during the ongoing war and enabling European solidarity imports.

Key Policy Issues & Investment Priorities

1 · Cernavodă units 3 & 4

The decision to complete Units 3&4 is the most consequential energy investment in Romania's post-communist history. The €7–8 billion project, backed by a US–Romania intergovernmental agreement and an EPC contract with AtkinsRéalis signed in 2024, would add approximately 1 400 MW of firm low-carbon capacity. Key risks include financing structure (SNN capital raise, export credit agencies, EU taxonomy eligibility), construction timeline certainty, and public acceptance. Success would position Romania as the SE European nuclear hub and dramatically improve its decarbonisation pathway.

2 · CE Oltenia coal transition

Romania committed under its National Energy & Climate Plan (PNIESC) to eliminate coal from electricity generation by 2032. CE Oltenia's restructuring plan — approved by the European Commission under state-aid rules — includes EU Just Transition Fund support and a phased closure of lignite plants at Rovinari, Turceni, and Ișalnița, replacing them with gas peakers and renewable capacity. With ~15 000 direct employees in Gorj and neighbouring counties, managing the social transition is as challenging as the technical one.

3 · Black Sea offshore wind

Romania holds one of Europe's largest theoretical offshore wind resources — estimated at up to 75 GW of technical potential in the Black Sea. A dedicated Offshore Wind Law (Law 23/2023) was adopted to create the permit and auction framework. However, grid connection from the Black Sea coast to load centres, shallow-water versus floating technology choices, seabed concession rules, and transmission planning by Transelectrica represent binding constraints. The first projects are not expected to commission before 2030–2032.

4 · Renewables acceleration & PNIESC targets

Romania's updated PNIESC targets 30.7% renewable energy share in final consumption by 2030, with a longer-term goal of 45% by 2050. Onshore wind expansion in Dobrogea is approaching land-use and grid capacity limits. Solar PV is growing rapidly. Competitive auctions (Contracts for Difference under the "capacity market" framework) are the primary support mechanism for new RE projects. Grid reinforcement — especially in the Dobrogea–Transylvania corridor — is a persistent bottleneck.

5 · Hidroelectrica as national champion model

The 2023 Hidroelectrica IPO — at ~€9 billion market capitalisation at listing, the largest in BVB history — demonstrated investor appetite for Romanian energy assets and offered a governance model combining state strategic control with minority private shareholders and market discipline. Transelectrica and other SOEs are watching closely. SNN is also partially listed, and there is ongoing debate about whether similar IPO structures could accelerate investment in CE Oltenia's successor assets.

Romania's PNIESC targets 30.7% renewable energy in final consumption by 2030 and aims for 45% by 2050. Combined with Cernavodă 3&4 and the eventual development of Black Sea offshore wind, Romania's low-carbon electricity share could exceed 70% of generation by the mid-2030s — contingent on investment decisions taken in 2024–2026.

Five Takeaways

1
Cernavodă 3&4 is the decade's defining investment — a €7–8 bn commitment that would make Romania the largest nuclear electricity producer in Southeast Europe and anchor its long-term decarbonisation trajectory.
2
Hidroelectrica's 2023 IPO created Romania's largest listed company and a regional hydro champion — demonstrating that partial privatisation of energy SOEs can raise capital, improve governance, and retain strategic state control simultaneously.
3
CE Oltenia's coal exit is the main social transition challenge — 15 000 jobs and several counties depend on lignite, and the 2030–2032 closure timeline is ambitious given the economic and political sensitivities involved.
4
Romania is a net exporter and could become a regional hub — its synchronous connections to four neighbours and its role supporting Moldova and Ukraine make it strategically important for SE European energy security.
5
Black Sea offshore wind (~75 GW theoretical) remains undeveloped — the regulatory framework and grid connection are the binding constraints; the first commercial projects are unlikely before 2030–2032, making early permitting decisions critical.
